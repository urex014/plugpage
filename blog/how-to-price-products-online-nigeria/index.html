How to price small chops, perfume, cakes and more online (simple formula)

"How much?" is the most common message any vendor gets. If you price too low, you work hard and make nothing. If you price by guessing, you can't explain your price when a customer pushes back. This guide gives you a simple formula you can use for small chops, perfume oils, cakes, clothes, hair, almost anything.

Important: all the naira figures below are made-up examples to show the maths. They are not market prices. Use your own receipts.

The formula

Price = (Product cost + Packaging + Overheads share + Your labour + Profit) + payment fee, with delivery charged separately

Let's break that down.

1. Product cost

Everything that goes into one unit: ingredients, fabric, oil, hair, bottles. If you buy in bulk, divide. For example, if one bulk purchase makes 16 bottles, divide the bulk cost by 16.

2. Packaging

Boxes, nylon, stickers, ribbons, cake boards, perfume boxes, thank-you cards. Small items add up fast. Count them per order.

3. Overheads share

Costs you pay whether or not you sell: gas or fuel for the generator, data, phone bills, market transport, the ring light you bought. Add up a month's overheads and divide by how many orders you usually do in a month. That's what each order has to carry.

4. Your labour

This is the one most vendors skip. Decide what an hour of your time is worth and multiply by the hours the order takes, including shopping, baking or sewing, packaging and answering DMs. If you don't pay yourself, your business is borrowing from you.

5. Profit

Profit is what's left for the business after you've paid for everything, including yourself. It's what lets you restock, survive a slow month and grow.

Markup vs margin (they're not the same)

  • Markup is profit as a percentage of cost. Cost ₦10,000, sell ₦13,000 → 30% markup.
  • Margin is profit as a percentage of the selling price. Same example → ₦3,000 ÷ ₦13,000 ≈ 23% margin.

If you want a specific margin, use: Price = Cost ÷ (1 − margin). For a 25% margin on a ₦30,000 cost: ₦30,000 ÷ 0.75 = ₦40,000.

Don't forget payment fees

If customers pay you by card or bank transfer through a payment gateway, the gateway takes a fee. As an example, Paystack's published pricing for local transactions in Nigeria is 1.5% + ₦100, with the ₦100 waived on transactions under ₦2,500, and fees capped at ₦2,000 per transaction. International cards are 3.9% + ₦100.

To pass the fee on without losing money, use: Price to charge = (Amount you want + ₦100) ÷ 0.985 (for local payments of ₦2,500 and above, below the cap).

If customers pay by direct bank transfer into your account, there's no gateway fee, but always confirm the money in your bank app before releasing goods. See our guide on fake alert scams.

Worked example 1: a small chops tray (example figures)

ItemAmount
Ingredients₦12,000
Packaging (tray, foil, stickers)₦1,800
Overheads share (gas, data, transport)₦1,200
Labour (3 hours at ₦1,000/hour)₦3,000
Total cost₦18,000
Profit you want₦6,000
Amount you want to receive₦24,000

If the customer pays through Paystack: (₦24,000 + ₦100) ÷ 0.985 ≈ ₦24,467, so round up to ₦24,500. Check: 1.5% of ₦24,500 is ₦367.50, plus ₦100, so the fee is ₦467.50 and you receive ₦24,032.50. ✅

Worked example 2: perfume oil, 6 ml (example figures)

  • Bulk oil: ₦45,000 fills about 16 bottles of 6 ml → ₦2,813 per bottle.
  • Bottle, label and box: ₦700.
  • Total cost per bottle: ₦3,513.
  • Selling price: ₦6,000 → profit ₦2,487, about 41% margin.

A Paystack payment of ₦6,000 would cost 1.5% (₦90) + ₦100 = ₦190 in fees, so decide whether to absorb it or price at ₦6,200.

Worked example 3: an 8-inch celebration cake (example figures)

  • Ingredients ₦22,000 + box and board ₦3,000 + gas/electricity ₦2,500 + labour (4 hours at ₦2,000) ₦8,000 = ₦35,500 cost.
  • For a 25% margin: ₦35,500 ÷ 0.75 = ₦47,333 → round to ₦48,000.

Delivery: charge it separately

Delivery costs change with distance, traffic and fuel prices, so don't bury them in your product price. Publish delivery fees by zone (for example "Mainland zone A", "Island", "Outside Lagos") and update them when riders' prices change. Customers argue less when the item price and the delivery fee are shown separately.

Quick checklist

  1. List the product cost per unit (divide bulk buys).
  2. Add packaging per order.
  3. Add your monthly overheads ÷ monthly orders.
  4. Pay yourself: hours × your hourly rate.
  5. Add profit, or use Price = Cost ÷ (1 − margin).
  6. Add the payment fee if you use a gateway.
  7. Charge delivery separately, by zone.
  8. Review prices whenever your supplier's prices change.
